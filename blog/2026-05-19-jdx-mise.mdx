Based on its name and location in the aqua backend, this function likely orchestrates the full installation lifecycle for tools managed via the Aqua registry — fetching, verifying, and placing binaries. With a cyclomatic complexity of 22, a max nesting depth of 11, and fan-out of 15, it carries all five flagged antipatterns: complex branching, deep nesting, multiple exit paths, god-function coupling, and excessive length. It sits in the fire quadrant with 2 commits in the last 30 days and was last changed just 1 day ago, meaning structural risk and active churn are colliding right now — a precise recipe for regression.

**Recommendation:** Add characterization tests that cover the major exit paths before touching this function further, then use extract-method refactoring to pull the distinct installation phases (download, verify, place, register) into their own focused functions, targeting a CC below 10 per extracted unit.

### `resolve` — src/config/env_directive/mod.rs

From its name and path, this function likely interprets and resolves environment directives defined in mise configuration files — a responsibility that naturally accumulates branching logic as the set of supported directive types grows. A cyclomatic complexity of 50 means 50 independent execution paths, each a required test case and a potential bug surface; at fan-out of 24, a misread of any one directive type can ripple into two dozen downstream callees. Like `install`, it is fire-quadrant with a recent activity score of 17.46, 2 touches in the last 30 days, and a last-change just 4 days ago — structural debt and live development pressure are compounding simultaneously.

**Recommendation:** Introduce a dispatch table or match-arm pattern to replace flat conditional chains, reducing the cyclomatic complexity by grouping directive-type handling into dedicated resolver functions; review the 24 fan-out targets first to understand which callees are most sensitive to behavioral changes here.
