### `_logistic_regression_path` — _logistic.py

<FunctionCard fn='_logistic_regression_path' file='sklearn/linear_model/_logistic.py' risk='3.22' band='critical' cc='69' nd='4' fo='56' touches='39' />

<MetricBar label='Cyclomatic Complexity' value='69' threshold='10' max='80' />

I would start with `_logistic_regression_path` because it combines a cyclomatic complexity of 69 with a fan-out of 56 and was last changed 6 days ago. From the docstring, it computes a logistic regression model across a list of regularization strengths and reuses the previous solution to warm-start the next one. Its signature takes more than 20 parameters, including the solver, the penalty mix, sample weights, thread count, and a callback context. That parameter surface is a large part of why the path count is so high.

Nesting depth is only 4, so the complexity is breadth, not depth: many independent branches at a shallow level, probably one per solver, penalty, or option combination. A fan-out of 56 means the function touches a very wide set of collaborators, and in Python, where types resolve at runtime, the real coupling is likely broader than the number suggests. The docstring also marks `Cs` as deprecated since 1.10 with removal in 1.14, which means the function is carrying a migration shim alongside its normal logic.

My recommendation: separate the argument normalization and deprecation handling from the solver dispatch, then give each solver branch its own helper. Each helper can be tested against a fixed input, and the dispatcher shrinks to something reviewable.

### `fit` — _logistic.py

<FunctionCard fn='fit' file='sklearn/linear_model/_logistic.py' risk='3.12' band='critical' cc='36' nd='2' fo='33' touches='39' />

The `fit` method has a cyclomatic complexity of 36, a nesting depth of only 2, and a fan-out of 33, with an activity-weighted risk score of 3.12. This is the public entry point for the estimator, so every caller of the logistic regression estimator inherits a change here. The excerpt shows a good deal of the branching is parameter reconciliation: a deprecated `C` checked against `alpha`, a deprecated `intercept_scaling` that emits a FutureWarning, and a special case that forces `l1_ratio` to 0 in certain conditions. After that come solver checking, array-namespace and device handling, and input validation.

The flat nesting is a point in its favor, since the code reads top to bottom. The cost is that two deprecation paths with different removal versions (1.14 for `C`, 1.12 for `intercept_scaling`) are interleaved with the main fitting logic. Those paths will disappear on a known schedule, and `fit` will get simpler when they do. Until then, I would pull the deprecation and parameter resolution into one private helper so the removals become a deletion in one place instead of surgery across a 36-path method.

### `_log_reg_scoring_path` — _logistic.py

<FunctionCard fn='_log_reg_scoring_path' file='sklearn/linear_model/_logistic.py' risk='2.98' band='critical' cc='15' nd='3' fo='23' touches='39' />

`_log_reg_scoring_path` is the smallest of the three by path count, with a cyclomatic complexity of 15, a nesting depth of 3, and a fan-out of 23, but it still lands in the critical band with a risk score of 2.98. By name and docstring, it scores models across the regularization path, which is the cross-validation side of the same machinery that `_logistic_regression_path` implements. It takes train and test indices, a scoring argument, and a `score_params` argument, and it carries the same deprecated `Cs` parameter.

The practical concern is coupling between the two path functions. They share a parameter vocabulary and a deprecation timeline, so a change to one is likely to require a matching change to the other. I would review them together, and I would not refactor one without a plan for the other. Its moderate size makes it a reasonable place to try extracting shared argument handling first.

### `_check_targets` — _classification.py

<FunctionCard fn='_check_targets' file='sklearn/metrics/_classification.py' risk='2.94' band='critical' cc='16' nd='3' fo='20' touches='35' />

Outside the logistic module, `_check_targets` in sklearn/metrics/_classification.py has a risk score of 2.94, a cyclomatic complexity of 16, and a fan-out of 20. It was changed today, with 35 touches in the last year and 3 distinct authors in the last 90 days. The docstring says it verifies that `y_true` and `y_pred` belong to the same classification task and raises a ValueError for mismatched lengths, mixed multilabel and multiclass targets, continuous or multioutput targets, and mixed string and integer labels.

That is a long list of rejection conditions, which accounts for the path count. The excerpt also shows it moving inputs to a chosen array namespace and device before validating, so there is an array-API layer sitting on top of the validation rules. A validator like this is called by many metric functions, so its blast radius extends to everything that builds on it. The file-level bug-fix rate is 29%, lower than the logistic file, with no reverts.

My recommendation: split the namespace and device preparation from the target-type checks. The type-compatibility rules can then be tested as a pure table of input combinations, independent of array backends.

### `multilabel_confusion_matrix` — _classification.py

<FunctionCard fn='multilabel_confusion_matrix' file='sklearn/metrics/_classification.py' risk='2.93' band='critical' cc='21' nd='3' fo='24' touches='35' />

`multilabel_confusion_matrix` is a public metric with a cyclomatic complexity of 21, a nesting depth of 3, and a fan-out of 24, scoring 2.93. Its docstring describes two modes, class-wise by default and sample-wise when `samplewise=True`, and says multiclass input is treated as binarized one-vs-rest. Supporting both modes, plus multilabel indicators, multiclass data, sparse matrices, optional label selection, and sample weights, is a plausible source of its branching.

Because it is public API, a regression here reaches users directly in their evaluation numbers. It shares its file's history with `_check_targets`: 35 touches in the last year, a bug-fix rate of 29%, and no reverts. I would extract the class-wise and sample-wise paths into separate helpers behind the existing signature so the public interface does not change.

## Where to start

I would take the three _logistic.py functions as one review unit and sequence the work: first the deprecation and parameter handling in `fit` and `_logistic_regression_path`, since those paths have known removal versions and will shrink on their own schedule, then the solver dispatch inside `_logistic_regression_path`, which is where the cyclomatic complexity of 69 sits. The two _classification.py functions are a smaller, separate job: split array-backend preparation from the label-type rules in `_check_targets`, then separate the two modes in `multilabel_confusion_matrix`.

For context, the quiet critical functions in the broader data score far lower than these five, and I would not spend the next review cycle on them.
