The riskiest function I found in d3, `render` in docs/components/PlotRender.js, hasn't been changed in 1,226 days. It carries an activity-weighted risk score of 9.02 and is the only critical-band entry out of 73 functions analyzed. All five top hotspots are 'debt' quadrant, with zero touches in the last year, so I read this as dormant debt rather than an emergency: the next developer to open these files inherits code nobody has looked at in over three years. I would start with `render` because it combines a fan-out of 12 with a long, multi-branch body, and every other function in the top five is in the docs components or the docs link-check test.

The table below ranks functions by **activity-weighted risk** — a score that multiplies structural complexity by recent commit frequency. A function that is both hard to understand (high cyclomatic complexity) and actively changing is a higher priority than one that is complex but untouched. CC = cyclomatic complexity (independent execution paths); ND = max nesting depth; FO = fan-out (distinct callees).

The distribution is lopsided in a reassuring way. Of 73 functions, 68 land in the 'ok' quadrant, none are 'fire', and the five 'debt' entries are the whole story. That means I am not flagging anything as a live regression risk. What I am flagging is that the highest-ranked code in this repository is dormant, moderately complex, and concentrated in docs infrastructure. Because none of it is changing, the risk is not that it breaks today; it is that the first change in years lands on code nobody remembers.

One caveat on the data: the file-level history is thin. Each of these files shows only one or two commits in total, no bug-linked commits, no reverts, and a bug-fix rate of 0%. So I have no historical defect signal to add. I am treating all five as maintainability debt, not as suspected defects.

### `render` — PlotRender.js

<FunctionCard fn='render' file='docs/components/PlotRender.js' risk='9.02' band='critical' cc='7' nd='3' fo='12' touches='0' />

This is the top-ranked function, with an activity-weighted risk score of 9.02, and it hasn't been changed in 1,226 days. Its cyclomatic complexity of 7 and nesting depth of 3 are modest on their own. What pushes it to critical is a fan-out of 12 combined with length. The excerpt shows a single method that handles a deferred-rendering path, an immediate in-browser path, and a server-side fallback path. Inside the deferred path it defines three closures (mounted, unmounted, and a disconnect helper) that manage an intersection observer and an idle callback.

That is the god-function and long-function pattern the analysis flagged: one method owns three rendering strategies plus lifecycle cleanup. The blast radius when someone next edits it is the problem. A change to the observer logic can interact with the mount and unmount closures, and the three branches share the same options object. My recommendation is extract-method. Pull the deferred path into its own function, move the observer setup and teardown into a small helper, and leave `render` as a dispatcher that picks a strategy. I would do that before anyone adds a fourth rendering mode.

### `quadtree_findVisited` — quadtreeFindVisited.js

<FunctionCard fn='quadtree_findVisited' file='docs/components/quadtreeFindVisited.js' risk='8.19' band='high' cc='12' nd='3' fo='4' touches='0' />

This one has the highest cyclomatic complexity in the top five at 12, a fan-out of 4, and 1,226 days since its last change, and it is listed in the table below. The excerpt shows a quadtree nearest-neighbor search that pushes quadrants onto a stack, prunes ones that cannot contain a closer node, subdivides the rest, and swaps the closest quadrant to the top of the stack. The pruning condition alone chains several comparisons into one expression, which is where much of the branching comes from.

The analysis tagged it as stale-complex, and I agree with the framing: it is a self-contained algorithm that hasn't moved and would be unpleasant to modify. The mixed-in mutation of the search bounds inside the leaf-visit branch is the kind of detail that is easy to break. Fan-out is only 4, so the coupling is low; the risk is in understanding it. I would add a focused test around the pruning and bound-shrinking behavior before changing anything, then consider naming the pruning predicate as its own function so the main loop reads as intent.

**`<anonymous>` and `readMarkdownFiles` — test/docs-test.js**

<FunctionCard fn='&lt;anonymous&gt;' file='test/docs-test.js' risk='7.33' band='high' cc='6' nd='2' fo='12' touches='0' />

<FunctionCard fn='readMarkdownFiles' file='test/docs-test.js' risk='7.24' band='high' cc='5' nd='3' fo='5' touches='0' />

These two share a file, so I am grouping them. The anonymous function is the test body for 'documentation links point to existing internal anchors'. It has a cyclomatic complexity of 12, a nesting depth of 2, and a fan-out of 12, and it has gone 1,225 days without a change. It crawls the docs directory, collects anchors and links, and then loops over every link to report broken ones. The fan-out of 12 comes from the crawl, parse, and assertion steps all living in one test body.

`readMarkdownFiles` is the recursive directory walker the test depends on. It scores 7.24 with a cyclomatic complexity of 5, nesting depth of 3, fan-out of 5, and 1,226 days since last change. The nesting comes from a loop containing a conditional that either recurses or yields.

Neither of these concerns me as production risk. They are test helpers, and their complexity is modest. The reason they rank is the fan-out and the long dormancy, not tangled logic. If I were tidying, I would split the anonymous test into a link-collection step and a validation step, so a failure points at one phase. The walker is fine as is; I would leave it alone unless the docs structure changes.

### `quadtree_visitParent` — quadtreeVisitParent.js

<FunctionCard fn='quadtree_visitParent' file='docs/components/quadtreeVisitParent.js' risk='6.82' band='high' cc='9' nd='2' fo='3' touches='0' />

The lowest-ranked of the five, with a cyclomatic complexity of 9, nesting depth of 2, fan-out of 3, and 1,226 days since last change. The excerpt shows an iterative quadtree traversal that walks a stack and, at each node, reports the parent's bounds to a callback before pushing up to four children. The branching comes from the four near-identical child checks.

This is the most benign of the group. The repeated child-push lines are a candidate for a small loop over the four quadrant offsets, which would cut the branch count noticeably. But with no recent activity and no defect history, I would treat that as opportunistic cleanup for whoever next touches the file, not a scheduled task.

**What I would do with this**

If I had one afternoon, I would spend it on `render`, because it is the only critical entry and the only one with a real extract-method case. The two quadtree functions are worth a test before any edit, and the link-check test is worth splitting when someone next needs to extend it. All of it is docs-layer code, so the library itself does not appear in the top five.
