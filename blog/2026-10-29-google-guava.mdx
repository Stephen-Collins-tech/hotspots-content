One file, LocalCache.java, holds all 5 of my top hotspots, spread across two copies (the guava and android source trees). Anyone touching the cache internals is working in the densest risk cluster in the repository. The top function, lockedGetOrLoad, is a 'debt' function with an activity-weighted risk score of 39.56 and 0 touches in the last year, so I am framing it as structural debt with high blast radius when next changed, not as live churn. Guava is a Java core library with 45,259 analyzed functions, 432 of them critical. I would start with the cache's locked load and store paths, where nesting depth, wide fan-out, and lock-protected state pile up together.

The table below ranks functions by **activity-weighted risk** — a score that multiplies structural complexity by recent commit frequency. A function that is both hard to understand (high cyclomatic complexity) and actively changing is a higher priority than one that is complex but untouched. CC = cyclomatic complexity (independent execution paths); ND = max nesting depth; FO = fan-out (distinct callees).

### `lockedGetOrLoad` — LocalCache.java

<FunctionCard fn='lockedGetOrLoad' file='guava/src/com/google/common/cache/LocalCache.java' risk='39.56' band='debt' cc='9' nd='6' fo='28' touches='0' />

I would start with `lockedGetOrLoad`, which pairs a nesting depth of 6 with a fan-out of 28; the android copy scores almost the same (an activity-weighted risk score of 38.68). Its cyclomatic complexity of 9 is moderate, so the trouble is not the number of paths but the depth and breadth around them. The excerpt shows a lock acquired and released in a try/finally, a scan down a hash bucket chain with key-equivalence checks, branches for entries that are loading, collected, or expired, and then a separate load step synchronized on the entry. It is flagged as deeply nested, god function, and long function, and the excerpt backs all three: lock management, table lookup, expiry handling, notification enqueuing, and load coordination share one body.

<MetricBar label='Fan-out' value='28' threshold='15' max='30' />

The highest-value first step is extract-method. I would pull the bucket scan into a helper that returns what was found, and move the loading-or-wait decision out of the locked region's body so each piece can be tested on its own. The ordering of lock, cleanup, and unlock is what I would protect with tests before moving anything. This function has gone 62 days without a change, so those tests can be written before anyone is under delivery pressure.

### `storeLoadedValue` — LocalCache.java

<FunctionCard fn='storeLoadedValue' file='guava/src/com/google/common/cache/LocalCache.java' risk='38.19' band='debt' cc='11' nd='5' fo='24' touches='0' />

`storeLoadedValue` scores 38.19 in the guava tree and 37.32 in the android tree, with a cyclomatic complexity of 11, nesting depth of 5, and fan-out of 24. It is flagged as complex branching, deeply nested, and god function. From the excerpt, it takes the lock, optionally expands the table when the count passes the threshold, walks the bucket chain, and then picks one of three outcomes: replace the loading reference, treat the loaded value as already overwritten, or insert a fresh entry. Each outcome does its own bookkeeping for counts, modification tracking, removal notifications, and eviction.

This branching is where a future change is most likely to go wrong, because the three outcomes share state updates that must stay consistent. I would decompose by outcome: one helper per branch, each owning its notification and count updates, with the outer method reduced to lock, lookup, and dispatch. Like its sibling, it has gone 62 days without a change, so it carries a high blast radius when next touched.

### `loadAll` — LocalCache.java

<FunctionCard fn='loadAll' file='guava/src/com/google/common/cache/LocalCache.java' risk='37.45' band='debt' cc='11' nd='2' fo='14' touches='0' />

`loadAll` is the outlier here. With nesting depth of only 2 and fan-out of 14, it is structurally flat next to the other two. Its risk comes from a cyclomatic complexity of 11 driven by exits. The excerpt shows a stack of catch clauses that each translate a different failure into a different exception type, a stats-recording finally block, and several early throws for null maps, null keys, and null values. That matches its exit-heavy flag. Every exit path is a test case, and some depend on the loader returning unusual results.

I would not refactor this one first. It is the cheapest function in the list to cover with tests, since each exit can be triggered with a stub loader, and that coverage is the useful step. If the exception translation ever needs to change, pulling the catch ladder into a single translation helper would shrink the method and put the mapping rules in one place. It has gone 55 days without a change.

**Same functions, android tree**

The android copies of `lockedGetOrLoad` and `storeLoadedValue` match the guava copies in complexity, nesting, and fan-out, and differ only slightly in risk score (38.68 and 37.32). With two near-identical copies of this logic, any refactor must be applied twice, and any behavioral fix risks landing in only one tree. I would treat the pair as a single work item and confirm the shared tests run against both before changing either.
