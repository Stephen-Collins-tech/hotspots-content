Across 1040 functions in hyper at commit b073378, 43 are critical and 19 are in the 'fire' quadrant; I would start with `role.rs` and then move to the two dispatcher loops in `src/proto/h1/dispatch.rs`.

**`parse` (CC 42) — role.rs**

<FunctionCard fn='parse' file='src/proto/h1/role.rs' risk='17.34' band='fire' cc='42' nd='5' fo='27' touches='2' />

This is the first of the two `parse` functions in `src/proto/h1/role.rs`, and the source excerpt shows it handling an HTTP/1 request line and headers. It takes a mutable byte buffer plus a parse context and returns a parse result for a request line. I would start here because it combines a CC of 42, a fan-out of 27, an activity-weighted risk score of 17.34, 2 touches in the last year, and only 8 days since last change. That is the live-risk profile: complex and being edited right now.

The excerpt explains the metrics. It sets up header storage with uninitialized memory in a small-vector, chooses capacity based on an optional configured maximum, then matches on the parser's result with separate arms for a complete parse, a partial parse and several error cases. Inside the complete arm it checks header size limits, URI length limits, method parsing and HTTP version, each with its own early return. That is a lot of exit paths, and each is a test case. Fan-out of 27 means the function depends on a wide surface of collaborators, so changes to header recording, method parsing or limit checks can ripple into it.

There is also unsafe-adjacent territory to flag. The code deliberately uses `MaybeUninit` storage, and a comment explains the choice to skip zeroing for a benchmark gain. I will not claim anything is wrong there, but uninitialized-memory handling inside a CC-42 function is exactly where I want a reviewer to state the invariant that nothing is read before the parser fills it. At file level, the signals are moderate: a bug-fix rate of 50% across 16 commits, 7 distinct authors in the last 90 days, zero bug-linked commits, zero reverts, and a review-comment density of 4.125. The wide author spread is the part I would watch in a function this size.

My recommendation is to extract the complete-parse arm into its own function that takes the parsed request and returns the validated pieces (length, method, path range, version), and to isolate the header-storage setup behind a small helper. That separates 'allocate scratch space' from 'validate and interpret', and it would take a meaningful share of the branches out of the top-level function.

**`parse` (CC 57) — role.rs**

<FunctionCard fn='parse' file='src/proto/h1/role.rs' risk='16.91' band='fire' cc='57' nd='4' fo='25' touches='2' />

The second `parse` has the highest cyclomatic complexity of anything in my top five: CC 57, with nesting of only 4 and fan-out of 25. The excerpt provided for this entry is identical to the first, so I cannot say what drives the extra 15 paths beyond what the metrics show. What I can say is that a CC of 57 is high by any threshold and means 57 independent paths through one function, each a potential bug surface and a required test case. It shares the same file-level context: 8 days since last change, 2 touches in the last year, an activity-weighted risk score of 16.91, and the same 7 recent authors.

The low nesting relative to complexity tells me the paths are mostly wide rather than deep, the shape you get from long sequences of independent checks. That pattern is usually easier to split than a deeply nested one. I would review it together with the previous function: list every early return across both `parse` functions, group them by what they reject (size, URI, method, version, header validity), and look for checks that can move into shared validation helpers. If both implementations repeat the same limit checks, centralizing them lowers both complexity scores at once.

**`poll_read` — dispatch.rs**

<FunctionCard fn='poll_read' file='src/proto/h1/dispatch.rs' risk='18.04' band='debt' cc='32' nd='10' fo='15' touches='0' />

`poll_read` has the highest activity-weighted risk score in the whole list at 18.04, even though it is in the 'debt' quadrant: 0 touches in the last year and 153 days since last change. Structure drives the score, not churn. A nesting depth of 10 is well past the point where I consider code hard to reason about, and CC 32 with fan-out 15 sits on top of it.

The excerpt shows an outer loop that checks closing state, then whether a request head can be read, then whether a body sender exists and the connection can read a body. Inside that, it polls the body receiver for readiness, polls the connection for a body frame, and branches on data frames, trailers and unknown frames, each with its own handling of the case where the receiver was dropped. It mixes `Poll::Pending` early returns with state that must be put back (the body sender is taken, then set again on several paths). That take-and-restore pattern is where I would look first: every path that returns or continues must leave the sender in the right state, and a missed restore is an easy mistake at a nesting depth of 10.

File-level signals: a bug-fix rate of 73% across 11 commits in the file, zero bug-linked commits, zero reverts, 5 distinct authors in the last 90 days, and a review-comment density of 2.4. A 73% bug-fix rate on a small commit history is context for review priority, not proof that this function is defective. My recommendation is extract-method on the body-reading branch: pull the frame handling (data, trailers, unknown) into a helper that returns a small enum such as 'keep sender', 'close read' or 'send error', so the loop body only decides what to do next. That would cut nesting from 10 to something I could hold in my head.

**`poll_write` — dispatch.rs**

<FunctionCard fn='poll_write' file='src/proto/h1/dispatch.rs' risk='17.65' band='debt' cc='21' nd='9' fo='19' touches='0' />

`poll_write` mirrors `poll_read`: CC 21, nesting 9, fan-out 19, 0 touches in the last year and 59 days since last change, with an activity-weighted risk score of 17.65. It is also 'debt', so the framing is the same: high blast radius when next changed, not live churn. It is the more recently modified of the two dispatcher loops, but still dormant by the standard of the 'fire' functions in `role.rs`.

The excerpt shows a loop that handles closing state, then writes a new message head (including choosing a known or unknown body length from the body's size hint), then flushes when the buffer is full, then writes body frames with a guard object that tracks whether the body should be cleared. A comment in the code itself notes that a new scope is needed there. Frame handling repeats the shape of the read side: data, trailers, unknown, with empty-chunk and end-of-stream special cases.

Fan-out of 19 is higher than `poll_read` despite lower CC, meaning this function leans on more collaborators per branch. The guard's clear-body flag is set on several paths, and I would want a test for each one. My recommendation is the same decomposition as the read side: split 'write a new head' from 'write a body frame' into separate functions, and make the guard-clearing decision a returned value instead of a flag mutated from several places. Because `poll_read` and `poll_write` share a file, a shared helper vocabulary for 'frame outcome' would make them easier to review together.

**What I would do with this list**

`encode_headers` in `src/proto/h1/role.rs` has CC 55, nesting 6 and fan-out 19, with an activity-weighted risk score of 16.7, 0 touches in the last year and 67 days since last change. It is in the 'debt' quadrant, so it belongs with the dispatcher loops as scheduled work.

The two `parse` functions are the only items I would treat as in-flight risk, because they were changed 8 days ago and have the most recent authors. The three 'debt' functions are the ones I would schedule: nothing is pushing them to change this week, which is exactly the window when refactoring costs least.
