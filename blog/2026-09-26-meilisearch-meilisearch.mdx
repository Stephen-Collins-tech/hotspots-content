Across 6,858 functions in meilisearch, 467 land in the critical band. The single riskiest, `extract_vector_points`, was edited the same day I pulled this data (0 days since last change), carrying a cyclomatic complexity of 26, a nesting depth of 7, and a fan-out of 36 — an activity-weighted risk score of 18.8. That combination is worth prioritizing: not a function I'd schedule for 'someday' cleanup. Three of the next four hotspots are structural debt sitting dormant in the document-indexing and scheduling path instead — `write_typed_chunk_into_index` (cyclomatic complexity 91, untouched for 64 days) and `extract_fid_docid_facet_values` (cyclomatic complexity 50, untouched for 258 days) are the two most extreme examples. The risk there isn't careless change; it's the large blast radius sitting untouched, waiting for the next person who needs to work with it.

The table below ranks functions by **activity-weighted risk** — a score that multiplies structural complexity by recent commit frequency. A function that is both hard to understand (high cyclomatic complexity) and actively changing is a higher priority than one that is complex but untouched. CC = cyclomatic complexity (independent execution paths); ND = max nesting depth; FO = fan-out (distinct callees).

### `extract_vector_points` — crates/milli/src/update/index_documents/extract/extract_vector_points.rs

<FunctionCard fn='extract_vector_points' file='extract_vector_points.rs' risk='18.8' band='critical' cc='26' nd='7' fo='36' touches='1' />

This is one of two 'fire' quadrant functions in the top five, and the excerpt shows why the numbers land where they do. The function walks the embedder settings diff, rebuilds per-embedder writers (manual vectors, prompts, inputs, removals) inside a loop, and branches on reindex actions with a nested match inside a match — that's where the nesting depth of 7 comes from. A fan-out of 36 means it's coordinating a large number of collaborators: grenad writers, embedder configs, fragment diffs, error logging. It had 1 touch in the last 30 days and was modified the same day as this snapshot, so whatever's being adjusted here is live. The file's history shows a bug-fix rate of 25% across 4 total commits with a single author in the last 90 days — not evidence of a defect today, but a signal that this code has needed correction before. My recommendation: pull the per-embedder writer setup and the fragment-diff resolution into their own helper functions before the next change lands, so the control flow that actually matters (the reindex action match) isn't buried in setup code.

### `write_typed_chunk_into_index` — crates/milli/src/update/index_documents/typed_chunk.rs

This is the highest cyclomatic complexity in the entire top five — 91 independent paths through one function — and it hasn't changed in 64 days, which puts it squarely in the debt quadrant: dormant structural risk. The excerpt shows a top-level match over `TypedChunk` variants (documents, and presumably several sibling chunk types not shown in the excerpt), and inside the documents arm alone there's a nested match handling vector-field parsing with an explicit early-break label (`'vectors:`). That shape — one branch per chunk type, each with its own sub-logic — is consistent with the high cyclomatic complexity here. A fan-out of 39 means a huge number of direct dependencies — LMDB writes, merger builders, del-add readers, embedder config lookups. The file shows a bug-fix rate of 50% across only 2 recorded commits, worth noting but too small a sample to draw a strong conclusion from. Given it's dormant now, the actionable move is to split this by `TypedChunk` variant into one function per case before the next feature touches it — 91 paths in one function is not something a reviewer can hold in their head during a diff, and 64 days of silence mean nobody currently holds that context fresh either.
